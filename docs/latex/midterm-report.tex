\textbf{Summary.} Redactor lets you pick an object once, by outlining it, drawing a box, or typing
a description, and then keeps it hidden for the rest of the video. At the proposal, our classical
tracker (GrabCut plus optical flow) broke as soon as the target shrank, went behind something, or
the shot cut. Since then we've done four more iterations. We fixed scale with a similarity-transform
tracker and added appearance-based recovery. Then we switched to SAM~2 for real footage, and
when SAM~2 couldn't find a target again after a long occlusion, we put our recovery back inside it.
On our DAVIS subset, the share of the target left uncovered dropped from 54\% with the original
tracker to 2.2\% with SAM~2. Most recently we tried removing the target outright and filling in
the background from other frames of the shot. On a real crosswalk clip that cut reconstruction
error by 60\% compared with single-frame inpainting. It still isn't perfect, so the workflow ends
with a person reviewing the video.

\section{Goals and audience}

We're building this for people who post footage of public places, like journalists, researchers,
and anyone filming a street or an event who needs to hide a bystander, a face, or a license plate.
Right now that means keyframing a blur by hand, shot by shot. We want a tool where you say once
what to hide and it stays hidden for the rest of the clip. Our priorities haven't changed since the
proposal. The worst failure is a leak, where the object shows up even for a single frame. After
that, we want the mask to stay tight, since blurring half the frame ruins the video too. We now
measure both, as the share of the target left uncovered and the extra frame area covered.

\section{Pipeline}

We kept the design from the proposal. Everything goes through one shared data type, the target
prompt, which is a rough region on some frame plus an instance id. The stages before the prompt
decide what to hide, and the stages after it figure out where that thing is in each frame and hide
it (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}). Most of what was planned in the proposal is now built.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Specify target.} The user outlines or boxes the object on any frame and checks a
mask preview before anything runs. Text descriptions go through Grounding DINO \cite{gdino}, which
returns boxes for a free-form phrase, and faces and plates use the same path.
\item \textbf{Segment.} GrabCut \cite{grabcut} or SAM~2 \cite{sam2} turns the rough prompt into a
pixel mask.
\item \textbf{Propagate.} This is either our classical tracker from Iteration~2 (similarity
transform from optical flow, GrabCut refinement, and appearance recovery) or SAM~2's video model
from Iterations~3 and 4.
\item \textbf{Redact.} We dilate the mask as a safety margin and cover it. As of Iteration~5 we can
also remove the object and fill the hole from other frames of the shot.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Why this design.} We wanted to be able to swap out one stage and measure it on its own,
and that works because the benchmark reads the mask between stages 3 and 4. It's how we compared
the classical tracker and SAM~2 without changing anything else. We wrote the selection tools, the
classical tracker, recovery and cut detection, multi-target support, corrections, coverage
margins, background reconstruction, the review and export app, and the benchmark. SAM~2 and
Grounding DINO are pretrained models we integrated, and we didn't train either one.

The biggest lesson so far is that our classical changes fixed the synthetic clips but didn't carry
over to real video (Figure~\ref{fig:results}). DAVIS IoU only moved from 0.298 to 0.310. SAM~2 got
to 0.835, but it had its own blind spot with long occlusions, and our classical recovery code ended
up being exactly what fixed it.

\textbf{What margins can't fix.} Figure~\ref{fig:margin} uses the same saved SAM~2 masks on DAVIS
with bigger safety margins. A 16-pixel margin brings the average uncovered area down to 0.74\%,
but the worst frame still leaves 25.15\% of the target uncovered. That frame is from
\texttt{bmx-trees}, where SAM~2 is confidently tracking only part of the rider, and no margin fixes
a mask in the wrong place. SAM~2 also didn't raise any warnings on the crosswalk even though we
measured misses there, so its confidence alone can't tell us which frames need review.

\section{Plan for the rest of the semester}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Denser real labels.} We'll label every crosswalk frame instead of six, and have a
second person check the labels. Then we can measure the worst miss and the longest uncovered
streak.
\item \textbf{Warning reliability.} We'll compare review warnings with actual misses on held-out
clips and count both missed and unnecessary warnings.
\item \textbf{Real occlusions.} Iteration~4 has only been tested on one synthetic occlusion. We'll
try real clips with repeated exits, reappearances, and lookalikes, and count wrong-person matches.
\item \textbf{Prompt evaluation.} We'll score text, face, and plate prompts on labeled frames by
detection recall at a fixed threshold.
\item \textbf{Removal quality.} We'll pick source frames more consistently over time and mask out
other moving people, to cut down on ghosting and flicker.
\item \textbf{Removal with recovery.} We'll run removal on top of Iteration~4's tracker and test it
on occluded targets, counting frames where the removed person pops back in.
\end{enumerate}
